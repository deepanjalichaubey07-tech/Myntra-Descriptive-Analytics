## 📌 Project Overview

This project applies **descriptive analytics** to a sample of **50 Myntra product listings** (July 2026) across five categories — Men T-Shirts, Men Shirts, Women Tops, Women Jeans and Women Heels (10 products each).

The dataset is a **cross-sectional catalog snapshot**, not sales or transaction data. The analysis therefore describes *listed-price patterns* only — not revenue, demand, conversion or profitability.

## 🎯 Objectives

1. Find the typical listed price of the selected products.
2. Identify the category with the highest average listed price.
3. Measure how widely prices vary across the sample.
4. Find the price band with the most products.
5. Compare price-band distributions for men's vs women's products.
6. Identify the category with the highest maximum price.
7. Identify the category with the greatest price dispersion.
8. Compare the collection-date snapshots.
9. Summarise the pricing pattern through KPIs.
10. Present the major descriptive findings.

## 🔄 Methodology

`Collect → Validate → Standardize → Derive price bands → Aggregate → Compare → Visualize → Interpret`

- Prices stored as numeric INR values; no missing or duplicate records.
- Price bands derived for frequency analysis and gender × price-band cross-tabulation.
- Tools: Python (pandas, matplotlib); calculations can also be reproduced in Microsoft Excel.
- No predictive model is used.

## 🔍 Key Findings

1. Overall mean listed price is ₹755.90 vs median ₹720.00.
2. **Men Shirts** have the highest category mean (₹999.80); **Women Tops** the lowest (₹469.70).
3. ₹400–699 is the most common band; the two middle bands hold **68%** of the sample.
4. The maximum price is ₹1,414 (Women Jeans).
5. **Women Heels** show the highest price dispersion (SD ₹270.96).
6. Collection-date averages (₹999.80 / ₹955.60 / ₹608.03) are driven by *which category was sampled on each date*, not by a real price trend.
7. The data supports descriptive catalog analysis only — not sales, demand or profitability conclusions.

## 💡 Recommendations

1. Use category-level price bands to ease catalog comparison.
2. Monitor high-dispersion categories for value-to-premium segmentation.
3. Repeat collection weekly/monthly for a true price-trend study.
4. Add units sold, order value, discount and final checkout price for commercial analysis.
5. Expand coverage to dresses, footwear, beauty and accessories.
6. Keep sampling rules consistent when comparing dates.
